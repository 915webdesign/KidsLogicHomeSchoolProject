# Twelve-week logic and decision course

Each week follows the routine in the [parent guide](parent-guide.md). The check is a conversation prompt, not a grade. Repeat or stretch a week as needed.

| Week | Idea | Play / decision task | Check for understanding |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Claims and reasons | Sort sentences into claim, reason, question; decide which game to play | “What is your claim, and why?” |
| 2 | Clear meanings | Define “fair” for a shared game; find two meanings | “Would your rule work in both examples?” |
| 3 | True, false, unknown | Mystery bag: distinguish what was seen from what was guessed | “What would we need to know?” |
| 4 | If–then rules | Make a card game rule; test a case where the rule applies | “Does the reverse rule follow?” Create project 1 |
| 5 | And, or, not | Build a treasure hunt with two conditions | “Does ‘or’ allow either or both?” |
| 6 | All, some, none | Sort toys; find a single counterexample to “all” | “Does one example prove ‘all’?” |
| 7 | Valid patterns | Use cards: If P then Q; P; therefore Q. Contrast with Q; therefore P | “Could Q happen another way?” |
| 8 | Evidence and sources | Compare two claims about a plant or toy; plan a safe test | “How reliable is the evidence?” Create project 2 |
| 9 | Causes and coincidences | Story: wet ground might be rain or sprinkler | “What else could cause it?” |
| 10 | Fair choices and tradeoffs | Choose a family activity with time and weather constraints | “Who is affected? What do we give up?” |
| 11 | Disagreement and uncertainty | Each side states the other's best reason before replying | “What would change your mind?” |
| 12 | Explain a decision | Revisit week 1 scenarios and present a chosen project | “Claim, reasons, alternative, uncertainty, next step?” Create project 3 |

## Three project options

1. **Rule game:** Design 3–5 rules, test edge cases, revise one rule.
2. **Mystery investigation:** Ask a safe question, collect observations, separate observations from guesses.
3. **Choice guide:** Compare two options with reasons, constraints, and a condition that could change the choice.

For Explorer level, use drawings, spoken explanations, and counters. For Builder level, add a truth table for two conditions, a counterexample, or a written argument. Both levels should explain their own idea before an AI coach offers suggestions.

## Decision routine

Use **CLEAR**: Clarify the choice; List options; Examine reasons and evidence; Ask for an alternative or counterexample; Revisit after new information. A choice can be reasonable without being certain.
