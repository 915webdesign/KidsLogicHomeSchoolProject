# Parent guide

## Rhythm and materials

One 25–40 minute meeting each week. Use paper, pencils, index cards, and ordinary household objects. Begin with a 5-minute story, spend 10 minutes on a game, 10 minutes on a decision challenge, and finish with a 5-minute reflection. Stop early while it is still fun.

Use the same idea at two levels: **Explorer** answers aloud with pictures or objects; **Builder** writes claims, reasons, and counterexamples. These are challenge levels, not age labels. Each child may switch levels at any time.

Ask, “What makes you think that?” and “What would change your mind?” Praise a revised answer and a kind question. Do not score a child on agreeing with an adult. A useful weekly record is: one claim, one reason, one question, and one changed thought. Keep children's records private.

## Before and after

In week 1, ask: “If the ground is wet, did it definitely rain?” and “We have 30 minutes; should we play outside or build indoors?” Record each child's reasoning privately. Repeat in week 12 and look for clearer reasons, alternatives, and uncertainty, rather than a higher tally of correct answers.

## Safety and AI

An adult starts and supervises every AI session. Children should not enter names, ages, location, school, photos, recordings, or private family details into an AI tool or this public repo. The AI may invent facts; check factual claims together. For decisions involving health, safety, money, or other people, the adult decides. If a child becomes frustrated, change to a physical game or pause.

## Teaching move

When an answer is wrong, ask for an example that tests it. Example: “All red things are apples.” A red toy car is a counterexample. Keep logic separate from character: an invalid argument can have a true conclusion, and a good person can make a reasoning error.

## Project cadence

At weeks 4, 8, and 12, make a small project. The learner chooses the topic and explains one decision. The adult handles GitHub access, reviews the result, and decides whether anything is published. Use anonymous project descriptions.
