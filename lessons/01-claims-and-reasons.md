# Lesson 1: Claims and reasons

**Goal:** Say what a claim is and give a reason that actually supports it. **Time:** 30 minutes. **Materials:** paper, two cups, cards with the sentences below.

## 1. Warm-up (5 minutes)

Say: “A claim is something we say might be true. A reason tells why we think so. A question asks for information.” Read: “The blue cup is bigger.” “Because it holds more water.” “How could we check?” Let each learner identify the claim, reason, and question. Fill the cups together if safe; appearance may mislead.

## 2. Sorting game (10 minutes)

Write one sentence per card:
- “We should play outside.”
- “The weather is dry.”
- “What time is it?”
- “This puzzle has a missing piece.”
- “I counted only 19 pieces, but the box says 20.”
- “Which game can we finish before dinner?”

Sort into claim, reason, or question. A reason can also be a claim that needs support. Ask learners to match reasons to claims and say when a reason is weak. For example, dry weather helps with playing outside, but it does not settle whether there is enough time.

## 3. Decision challenge (10 minutes)

Scenario: “We have 20 minutes before dinner. Option A is a 30-minute board game. Option B is a 10-minute drawing game.” Ask each child to choose and give a reason. Then add new information: “The board game can be saved halfway.” Invite a revision. Neither choice needs to be the teacher's choice.

Explorer: draw or point to options; complete “I choose __ because __.” Builder: give two reasons, one objection, and a reply.

## 4. Exit ticket (5 minutes)

Ask each learner: “What do you choose, why, and what question would help you decide?” Record privately, including any changed answer. Celebrate careful thinking.

## Optional browser games

Use [the Lesson 1 games](../games/README.md) to practice the same ideas: Statement Sorter, Reason Detective, and Dinner Countdown. Keep the adult-led cup warm-up and ask the learner to explain their thinking before checking an answer. The games offer Explorer and Builder prompts.
